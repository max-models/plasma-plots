[CoilStellaration](https://huggingface.co/datasets/proxima-fusion/coilstellaration) is a dataset of
optimized stellarator coilsets for the plasma boundaries of
[ConStellaration](https://huggingface.co/datasets/proxima-fusion/constellaration). A coilset is
stored by its unique coils: closed curves given by the Fourier coefficients of `x`, `y` and `z`
(the layout of DESC's `FourierXYZCoil`), one current per coil, the number of field periods and
whether it is stellarator symmetric. A boundary is a Fourier series of `R` and `Z`
(`SurfaceRZFourier`). plasma-plots reads both from the plain JSON the datasets store, without DESC
or the `coilstellaration` package:

```python
import plasma_plots
from plasma_plots.coils import coil_to_coil_distance, distance_to_surface

# every coil of the device, over (coil, s)
coils = plasma_plots.from_coilset(coilset)
# the plasma surface, with its minor radius
plasma = plasma_plots.from_boundary(boundary)
a = plasma.attrs["minor_radius"]

coils["plasma_distance"] = distance_to_surface(coils, plasma) / a
coils.plasma.plot.coils_3d(color_by="plasma_distance", surface=plasma).show()
```

## From the datasets

The `results` subset of CoilStellaration has one row per sample, with the IDs of its coilsets and
of its ConStellaration boundary. A coilset is fetched by ID through the subset's
`id_to_file_map.parquet`; the `json` of its row is what `from_coilset` reads. ConStellaration's
`boundary.json` is what `from_boundary` reads. This block is what
`scripts/fetch_coilstellaration_sample.py` runs to get the coilset drawn
[further down](#a-coilset-from-coilstellaration). It downloads from Hugging Face (about 0.7 GB,
mostly the boundary column), so it is not run when the docs are built:

```python
import json

import pandas as pd

coilstellaration = "hf://datasets/proxima-fusion/coilstellaration"
constellaration = "hf://datasets/proxima-fusion/constellaration"

results = pd.read_parquet(f"{coilstellaration}/results/eval-part-0.parquet")
# a third of the samples have no DESC coilset
has_desc = results["desc_coilset_id"].notna()
tight = results["benchmark/stratification"] == "tight"
chosen = results[has_desc & results["benchmark/fixed_shape_track"] & tight]
sample = chosen.sort_values("constellaration_boundary_id").iloc[0]

where = [("id", "==", sample["desc_coilset_id"])]
index = pd.read_parquet(
    f"{coilstellaration}/coilsets/id_to_file_map.parquet", filters=where
)
row = pd.read_parquet(
    f"{coilstellaration}/{index['path'].iloc[0]}", filters=where
)
coilset = json.loads(row["json"].iloc[0])

for part in range(3):
    rows = pd.read_parquet(
        f"{constellaration}/data/train-{part:05d}-of-00003.parquet",
        columns=["plasma_config_id", "boundary.json"],
        filters=[
            ("plasma_config_id", "==", sample["constellaration_boundary_id"])
        ],
    )
    if len(rows):
        boundary = json.loads(rows["boundary.json"].iloc[0])

coils = plasma_plots.from_coilset(coilset)
plasma = plasma_plots.from_boundary(boundary)
```

`from_coilset` also takes the `coilstellaration` library's `Coilset` and a DESC `CoilSet` of
`FourierXYZCoil` (`coilset.to_FourierXYZ()` converts other DESC coils); `from_boundary` also takes
ConStellaration's `SurfaceRZFourier`.

## Reading a coilset

`from_coilset(coilset, n_points=128)` expands the unique coils into every coil of the device as
DESC's `CoilSet` does: for a stellarator-symmetric coilset their mirror images in the second half
of the field period, then all of these rotated into every field period. Coil `i` of the Dataset is
coil `i` of DESC's expanded coilset (checked point by point in the tests).

| In the Dataset | What it is |
|---|---|
| dims `coil`, `s` | every coil of the device; the curve parameter `s` in `[0, 2π)` |
| coordinates `X`, `Y`, `Z` (m) | the points along each coil |
| coordinates `unique_coil`, `mirrored`, `field_period` | which stored coil each one copies, and how |
| `current` (A) | per coil, the current of the coil it copies |
| `length` (m) | per coil |
| `curvature` (1/m) | along each coil, exact from the Fourier series: `|r' × r''| / |r'|³` |
| attributes `nfp`, `stellarator_symmetric`, `fourier_order` | from the coilset |

## The plasma boundary

`from_boundary(boundary, theta=64, zeta=None)` evaluates
`R = Σ r_cos[m, n] cos(m θ − nfp n φ)`, `Z = Σ z_sin[m, n] sin(m θ − nfp n φ)` on a grid of `θ`
and the cylindrical angle `φ` (by default over the whole torus) and returns one surface in the
layout of [DESC's Datasets](/plasma-plots/guides/desc/): dims `rho` (the single value 1), `theta`,
`zeta`, coordinates `X`, `Y`, `Z` and the variable `R`. Its attributes hold the major radius and
the **minor radius** `a = sqrt(A / π)` of the mean cross-section area `A`, the length that
CoilStellaration's `normalized_*` metrics divide by.

`plasma.plasma.plot.boundary_sections()` (or `plasma_plots.coils.plot_boundary_sections`, which
also takes a `boundary.json` directly) draws the cross-sections in the `(R, Z)` plane at five
toroidal angles from `phi = 0` to half a field period, `π / N_fp`. This is the view
ConStellaration uses for its boundaries. For a stellarator-symmetric surface these angles show
every shape it takes. Here is the example boundary of the ConStellaration benchmark
(`hugging_face_competition/inputs/boundary.json` in the
[constellaration repository](https://github.com/proximafusion/constellaration), four field
periods, kept in `scripts/data`):

```python
import json

from plasma_plots.coils import plot_boundary_sections

boundary = json.loads(open("constellaration_boundary.json").read())
plot_boundary_sections(boundary)
```

## Distances, curvature and field error

The benchmark scores a coilset by four metrics. Three of them are geometry, and plasma-plots gives
them at every point of every coil, so they can be plotted where they are reached:

```python
a = plasma.attrs["minor_radius"]
coils["plasma_distance"] = distance_to_surface(coils, plasma) / a
coils["coil_distance"] = coil_to_coil_distance(coils) / a

float(coils.curvature.max()) * a  # normalized_coil_curvatures/max
# normalized_coil_to_plasma_min_distances/min
float(coils.plasma_distance.min())
float(coils.coil_distance.min())  # normalized_coil_to_coil_min_distances/min
```

Both distances are taken between sampled points, so they converge from above as the grids are
refined (`n_points` of the coils, `theta` and `zeta` of the surface). The fourth metric, the field
error `|B·n| / |B|` on the plasma, needs the field of the coils; DESC computes it:

```python
import numpy as np
import xarray as xr
from desc.grid import LinearGrid

surface = plasma_plots.from_desc(
    eq,
    "n_rho",
    rho=[1.0],
    theta=64,
    zeta=np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 240, endpoint=False),
)
points = np.stack([surface[axis].values.ravel() for axis in "XYZ"], axis=-1)
B = desc_coils.compute_magnetic_field(
    points, basis="xyz", source_grid=LinearGrid(N=64)
)
n = surface.n_rho  # the unit normal, Cartesian components along "component"
B = xr.DataArray(
    np.asarray(B).T.reshape(n.shape), dims=n.dims, coords=n.coords
)
field_error = abs((B * n).sum("component")) / np.sqrt((B**2).sum("component"))
```

The figures on this page use two coilsets around DESC's W7-X example, five coils per half
period. These are not W7-X's real coils. The first is a starting set
(`desc.coils.initialize_modular_coils`): identical **circles** around the magnetic axis, each in
the plane normal to the axis. A coil optimization begins from coils like these, so their field
error is large. The second is what a short DESC optimization makes of them: fifteen iterations
against the field error, with bounds on the curvature (bends no sharper than the minor radius),
the length and the distances to the plasma and between coils. The coils become non-planar, like
W7-X's own, and the field error drops by a factor of about fifty. A longer optimization, or
CoilStellaration's REGCOIL and DESC pipeline, goes further. The numbers, from the script at the end
of the page:

## In 3-D

`coils.plasma.plot.coils_3d()` (or `plasma_plots.pyvista_plots.pyvista_coils`) draws the coils as
tubes. `color_by` takes any variable over `coil` or `(coil, s)`, and `surface` the plasma: a
Dataset such as `from_boundary`'s is drawn in grey, a DataArray colored by its values
(`surface_clim` fixes its color limits, to compare scenes).

The first scene shows the **starting coils**, and they are circles on purpose. A coil
optimization needs a first guess, and the simplest one is a ring of identical planar circles
around the magnetic axis, each in the plane normal to the axis there. DESC's
`initialize_modular_coils` makes exactly that, and the optimization below starts from it.
Circles ignore the plasma's shape, so the field they make is not tangent to W7-X's surface: the
field error averages 12 % and reaches 35 %. Their field error on W7-X's last surface,
on a logarithmic scale:

```python
log_error = np.log10(field_error)
coils.plasma.plot.coils_3d(
    surface=log_error, surface_cmap="magma", surface_clim=(-4, 0)
).show()
```

<PyVistaScene src="/pyvista/coils_3d_field_error.html" image={fig_field_error} alt="Fifty circular planar coils around W7-X's last flux surface, colored by the normal field error on a logarithmic scale" />

The **optimized coils**, on the same scale: after fifteen iterations they bend around the
plasma, and the field error falls to 0.25 % on average (dark purple):

## A coilset from CoilStellaration

A real coilset of the dataset, fetched as [above](#from-the-datasets) and kept in
`scripts/data`: the DESC-optimized coilset of one benchmark instance of the `eval` split, in the
`fixed_shape` track (three field periods, five coils per half period) and the `tight` stratum, the
third of the instances with the smallest field error. Its plasma is a ConStellaration boundary,
in five cross-sections over half a field period:

![Cross-sections of the sample's ConStellaration boundary at five toroidal angles over half a field period](../../../assets/figures/coils_sample_sections.png)

The coils colored by their distance to the plasma, the plasma by the field error, on the same
scale as the W7-X coils above. The field error comes from DESC's Biot-Savart field of the coils
on `from_boundary`'s surface, with the normal from the surface points. The dataset's coils wind
far more than the fifteen-iteration W7-X coils above: they are what a full REGCOIL and DESC
optimization makes to meet tight targets on the field error and the distances.

<PyVistaScene src="/pyvista/coils_3d_coilstellaration.html" image={fig_sample} alt="A CoilStellaration coilset of thirty coils around its ConStellaration plasma with three field periods, the coils colored by their distance to the plasma and the plasma by the field error" />

The dataset's metrics of the coilset next to plasma-plots' own. The geometric ones agree to a few
percent; they are sampled on different grids, and the dataset measures from DESC's equilibrium of
the boundary. The mean field error depends on how the surface is sampled: here uniformly in the
boundary's own angles.
